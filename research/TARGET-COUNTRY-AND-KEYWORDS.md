# Hindi Teaching: Target Country + Local Keyword Map

For: a Hindi teacher in India who wants to teach students in tier-1 English-speaking countries.
Prepared 9 Nov 2026. Companion file: `keywords-by-country.csv` (86 keywords, paste-ready).

---

## The answer, up front

**Target Australia first. Then the USA (weekends only). Then the UK. New Zealand as a cheap third test.**

Not because Australia is the biggest market. It isn't. It wins on four things that actually decide whether this works:

1. **Timezones line up with her day.** Australian after-school hours are her late morning. American after-school hours are her 2am.
2. **Hindi is a formal school subject.** VCE Hindi (Victoria) and HSC Hindi Continuers (NSW) are real, examinable courses. Parents pay for exam outcomes, not for hobby learning.
3. **Price point is the best of the six.** Superprof's Australian Hindi listings show an average of **$25/hour**, with India-based tutors competing down at $9 to $14. There is room to be premium.
4. **Supply is fragmented and unbranded.** The Australian demand is being served by marketplace profiles (Superprof, AmazingTalker) and Indian teachers posting in Facebook groups. Nobody owns the search results for "VCE Hindi tutor". That is an open lane.

**Why the demand numbers look the way they do**

- Australia: **197,000+ Hindi speakers** at the 2021 Census, up from 159,652 in 2016. India-born population 673,352 and still the fastest-growing source country. Small numbers, high intent, high price.
- USA: the largest absolute market by far, but also the most organised competition. Outschool, CourseHorse and dedicated Hindi schools already rank for the kids keywords.
- UK: **Hindi is not among the top main languages** in England and Wales. Punjabi and Urdu sit at roughly 0.5% each and lead the South Asian languages. Hindi demand here is driven by IGCSE families more than by raw diaspora size.
- Canada: real demand, but concentrated in Punjabi-speaking families. Hindi is the second language in that household, not the first.
- New Zealand and Ireland: too small to build around. Worth a page each only because competition is close to zero.

## Timezone: the factor most people skip

Indian Standard Time is UTC+5:30. Comparison for the northern-hemisphere winter:

| Market | After-school slot locally | That time in India | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sydney / Melbourne | 4:00 to 7:00 pm | 10:30 am to 1:30 pm | Prime working hours |
| Perth | 4:00 to 7:00 pm | 1:30 to 4:30 pm | Prime working hours |
| Auckland | 4:00 to 7:00 pm | 8:30 to 11:30 am | Prime working hours |
| London | 4:00 to 7:00 pm | 9:30 pm to 12:30 am | Doable, but late |
| Toronto / New York | 4:00 to 7:00 pm | 2:30 to 5:30 am | Not viable |
| USA (weekend mornings) | 8:00 to 11:00 am Sat/Sun | 6:30 to 9:30 pm Sat/Sun | The only workable US slot |

This is the whole reason Australia ranks first despite being a fifth of the US market. She can teach Australian students in daylight and still take Indian students in the evening.

## Keyword strategy: win the lanes, not the head term

She will not rank for "learn hindi" or "hindi classes online". Those are dominated by India-based sites with years of authority, and the traffic is mostly people who will never pay a premium. Ignore the head terms.

Three lanes are winnable and convert:

**Lane 1: Geo-modified commercial terms.** "hindi classes melbourne", "hindi tutor sydney", "hindi tutor auckland". Local intent, low competition, buyers. This is where a new site can rank in 2 to 4 months.

**Lane 2: Exam-specific terms.** "vce hindi tutor", "hsc hindi continuers", "igcse hindi 0549". Tiny search volume, almost no specialist supply, and the highest willingness to pay in the entire market. One VCE student at $40/hour is worth twenty free-app users.

**Lane 3: Diaspora parents.** "hindi classes for kids", "hindi reading and writing for kids", "hindi for nri kids". This is the volume lane and the bulk of the revenue. Parents of 5 to 14 year olds searching in a language their child is losing.

**Local phrasing matters more than people think.** The same intent is typed differently in each country:

| India phrasing (what most competitor sites use) | Australia / US phrasing | Use this |
|---|---|---|
| hindi tuition | hindi classes, hindi tutor, hindi lessons | "classes", "tutor", "lessons" |
| home tuition | in-home or online tutor | "online tutor" |
| tuition centre | language school, learning centre | "language school" |
| spoken hindi | conversational hindi | "conversational" |

A site written in Indian English ("Hindi tuition classes near me with fees") reads as foreign to an Australian parent and converts badly, even when it ranks. Write for the local parent.

## Do not wait for SEO

Organic ranking for a brand-new site takes 3 to 6 months. She needs students before that. Run these in parallel from week one, because the demand is visibly sitting in public places already:

1. **Superprof and AmazingTalker profiles.** These are the pages currently ranking for her best keywords. Being listed there is faster than outranking them. Australian Hindi listings average $25/hour, India-based tutors charge $9 to $14, so she can position at $18 to $22 as the credentialed middle option.
2. **Diaspora Facebook groups.** Posts like "looking for a Hindi teacher for my kids in Sydney" appear constantly in Australian and UK Indian community groups. These are literal buyer requests with no SEO competition at all.
3. **School-adjacent channels.** VCE and HSC Hindi cohorts are small and tight. A single well-placed recommendation inside one Melbourne or Sydney school community is worth more than a month of content.

## Reality checks

- **Volume is small.** These are low-volume keywords in absolute terms. Realistic ceiling for the Australian opportunity is dozens of students, not thousands. That is fine for a solo teacher and not fine for a course platform.
- **Competition is real in the US.** Outschool and CourseHorse already own the kids keywords. Treat the US as weekend cohorts and marketplace listings, not as an SEO project.
- **The USA is a scheduling decision, not a marketing one.** Unless she is willing to teach 2:30 to 5:30 am IST, the US is weekend-only. Decide that before spending money on US content.
- **Not verified:** US Hindi speaker counts and per-country search volumes. See the Semrush note below.
